# Research Brief: Pair-Order Consistent, Uncertainty-Aware Semantic Change Detection

**Scope:** University of Moratuwa CSE group project (semester scope). Siamese CNN, binary change mask from two RGB frames (reference vs. edited video version, same semantic time, small camera/registration offset). Two claimed contributions: (A) bounded, change-gated feature alignment; (B) pair-order (swap) consistency loss, extended to appeared/disappeared direction.

---

## 0. Executive summary

Both contributions sit in well-populated but *not saturated* research territory. Nobody currently ships the **combination** "tanh/clamp-bounded, change-gated feature-space offset + an explicit swap-and-permute consistency loss with a signed-difference fusion trunk" as a single, ablated system aimed at generic RGB scene-edit detection (rather than satellite land-cover mapping). That combination, argued carefully, is your defensible novelty. Neither piece alone is new:

- **(A) Bounded alignment** — deformable/flow-based feature alignment for change detection is an active 2023–2025 sub-area (BiFA, RACDNet, DC-Mamba/BTDA, APD, ADMNet). The "alignment erases real change" failure mode is explicitly acknowledged in this literature and in the adjacent video-super-resolution/optical-flow literature, with known mitigations (offset regularization, gating, forward–backward consistency). A hard-bounded (tanh·max-disp) offset with an explicit change-gate is a real but incremental design choice — you must show it beats an *unbounded* or *ungated* ablation, or reviewers will call it decoration.
- **(B) Pair-order consistency** — the closest, most dangerous prior art is (i) **ChangeMask** (Zheng et al., ISPRS P&RS 2022), which builds "temporal symmetry" in as an architectural inductive bias via a Time-Symmetric Transformer, and (ii) a **just-posted (Jan 2026)** arXiv paper, *"Exchange Is All You Need for Remote Sensing Change Detection"* (Dong et al., arXiv:2601.07805), which formalizes bi-temporal exchange as an "orthogonal permutation operator" for the *binary* change branch. Neither extends this to a *directional* appeared/disappeared head with an explicit swap-and-permute loss, and neither is built on a signed-difference (antisymmetric) fusion trunk — most existing Siamese CD networks that care about symmetry simply pick a symmetric fusion function (`|a−b|`, `a+b`, `max`) and get invariance "for free" from weight sharing. Your team's use of **signed difference** is the fork in the road: it is *necessary* if you want the model to see directional information at all, but it makes order-consistency a **non-trivial, must-be-learned property**, not an architectural freebie. That precise distinction — and the explicit swap-then-permute loss for the 2-class direction head — is your cleanest, most citable novelty claim.

Read Sections 4 and 7 first if you only have time for two sections; that is where the reviewers will actually attack.

### 1.3 Explicitly *bounded* offsets in the literature

Direct "bounded offset for CD" precedent is thin — this is a genuine (small) opening:

- The DC-Mamba/BTDA gate (arXiv:2509.15563, 2025) is the closest hit: a lightweight offset-prediction head plus "a gating mechanism allow[ing] the network to adaptively weaken unnecessary deformations in well-aligned regions." This is a *soft, learned* gate, not a *hard numerical bound* on displacement.
- Outside CD, video super-resolution deformable-alignment papers explicitly discuss **offset overflow** as a known failure mode and motivate constraining offset magnitude: Chan, Wang, Yu, Dong, Loy, "Understanding Deformable Alignment in Video Super-Resolution," AAAI 2021, arXiv:2009.07265 (documents training instability from unconstrained offsets, "offset overflow… deteriorating the final performance," and proposes an "offset-fidelity loss" constraining offsets not to deviate far from optical flow); Chan, Zhou, Xie, Loy, "BasicVSR++," CVPR 2022, arXiv:2104.13371 (flow-guided deformable alignment — the flow acts as a *soft prior/regularizer* on the offset rather than a hard clamp).
- A `tanh(·) × max_disp` hard clamp (what you propose) does not appear to be published *inside a CD paper* specifically. This is a legitimate, citable engineering delta: "prior CD alignment modules regularize offset magnitude via loss terms or soft gates (Chan et al. 2021; DC-Mamba 2025); we instead impose a hard architectural bound via tanh-scaling, which guarantees an a-priori worst-case displacement irrespective of training dynamics — a property soft penalties cannot guarantee." That is a precise, honest, defensible framing (see §7).

## 2. THE CORE DANGER — alignment as a degenerate/collapsing solution

This is well-documented, just scattered across three adjacent literatures. Assemble the citation trail like this:

1. **Named directly in CD**: multiple sources state the paradox plainly — "while image registration aims to minimize spatial displacement, it may inadvertently 'force-align' regions that contain genuine changes, thereby suppressing true change signals" (recurring framing across the BiFA/RACDNet-adjacent literature, e.g. "Bridging the resolution gap: Semantic-aware alignment for cross-resolution change detection," *Pattern Recognition*, 2026, ScienceDirect S0031320326000166). This is your one-sentence motivating quote for contribution (A)'s introduction — but note it's presented in these papers as background problem-framing, not as an ablated finding; you will need to *produce* the ablated evidence, not just cite the intuition.
2. **Named in video super-resolution / optical flow** (deepest, most quantitative treatment of "alignment can go wrong" — mine this literature for your *mitigation* design):
   - **Offset magnitude regularization**: Chan et al. AAAI 2021 (arXiv:2009.07265) — "offset-fidelity loss" keeps learned offsets close to an optical-flow estimate; without it, offsets diverge ("offset overflow") and alignment quality collapses non-monotonically with more deformable groups.
   - **Occlusion/confidence masking**: standard in unsupervised flow — a predicted or forward-backward-derived occlusion mask multiplies the photometric/alignment loss so the network is not penalized (and does not try to force a match) where no correspondence exists. Canonical references: Hur & Roth, **"MirrorFlow: Exploiting Symmetries in Joint Optical Flow and Occlusion Estimation,"** ICCV 2017, arXiv:1708.05355; Zou, Luo, Huang, **"DF-Net: Unsupervised Joint Learning of Depth and Flow using Cross-Task Consistency,"** ECCV 2018, arXiv:1809.01649.
   - **Forward–backward consistency**: "traversing a flow vector forward and then backward should arrive at the same position" — used to derive an occlusion/validity mask that gates the loss (same two papers above; also the general unsupervised-flow literature, e.g. patch-consistency + occlusion estimation, *Computer Vision and Image Understanding*, 2020, ScienceDirect S0031320319304911). **Direct transfer to your setting**: warp frame-2 features to frame-1 with your predicted offset, then warp back with the negated/inverted offset (or a small backward head), and penalize (or gate) wherever the round-trip displacement is large — this doubles as both a regularizer *and* a diagnostic for where your alignment module is untrustworthy (candidate genuine-change region).
   - **Gating on a change/difference score** — directly your idea. This is the load-bearing design decision. Precedent for gating a *spatial transform* on a *content-derived confidence/attention* map exists in the CD literature already (DC-Mamba/BTDA's gate, above; CoSA's "learnable residual gating," arXiv:2606.21932); the honest framing is "we adopt an already-emerging pattern (gate the offset by a content signal) but make the bound and the gate target explicit and ablatable," not "we invented gating."
3. **The circularity risk you must pre-empt in your own design** (this is the finding a strong reviewer will produce if you don't produce it first): if the gate is conditioned on the *same, still-training* change-probability map the network is trying to predict, there is a **degenerate equilibrium**: (a) gate → 1 everywhere ⇒ alignment always fires ⇒ early in training, when the change head is unreliable, real changes get warped away and the loss will *reward* this because it initially reduces spurious pixel-level disagreement; or (b) the network could instead learn a change head that is over-conservative (predicts "no change" almost everywhere) specifically because doing so keeps the gate low and avoids the harder alignment-vs-change trade-off, which trivially minimizes a badly-weighted loss. **Recommended mitigation to propose and ablate**: derive the gate from a signal that is *not* the network's own change-probability output — e.g., a cheap, fixed low-level registration-confidence proxy (local normalized cross-correlation or SSIM patch score, or the *magnitude* of the raw pre-alignment feature difference, thresholded/smoothed), or use a stop-gradient on the gate's dependency on the change head so gradients from the alignment path cannot directly shape what the change head predicts. State this circularity risk explicitly in your proposal — reviewers respond very well to "we identified this failure mode and designed around it" versus having it pointed out to them.

## 3. PyTorch-level design for the bounded alignment module

### 3.1 Recommended implementation (concrete)

```python
# Offset head: small conv trunk -> 2*groups channels (dx, dy) per offset group
# Bound via tanh, then scale by a fixed max displacement (in pixels, at this feature-map scale)
offset_raw = self.offset_conv(torch.cat([f1, f2], dim=1))          # [B, 2*G, H, W]
offset = torch.tanh(offset_raw) * self.max_disp                     # hard bound, per-group

# Change-aware gate: derived from a signal decoupled from the (training) change head,
# e.g. local normalized cross-correlation / abs-diff magnitude, NOT the change logits directly
gate = self.gate_head(torch.cat([f1, f2.detach()], dim=1)).sigmoid()  # [B, 1, H, W] in [0,1]
offset = offset * gate                                               # gated + bounded

# Warp f2 onto f1's grid with grid_sample (flow-style) OR feed `offset` into deform_conv2d
f2_aligned = warp(f2, offset, align_corners=False, padding_mode='border')
```

- **Two implementation routes**, both viable at your scale:
  1. `torchvision.ops.deform_conv2d` (or `DeformConv2d` module) — treats the offset as *per-kernel-tap* sampling displacement inside a convolution; naturally fuses "align" and "re-convolve" into one op. Good if you want alignment fused with a learned re-weighting of the aligned neighborhood (DCNv2-style modulation mask is a bonus you get almost for free and can reuse as your "gate").
  2. Predict a single 2-channel dense flow field at each pyramid level and use `torch.nn.functional.grid_sample` to warp the whole feature map (PWC-Net/RAFT-style). Simpler to reason about, cheaper to bound (`tanh * max_disp` on a 2-channel tensor is trivial), and easier to add forward–backward consistency (just warp with `-offset`, or predict a second, small backward offset head and compare).
  - **Recommendation for a semester project**: start with route (2) — `grid_sample`-based warping. It is simpler to debug, visualize (dump the flow field as a color wheel per training step), and bound. Only move to `deform_conv2d` if you want the DCNv2 modulation mask specifically and have time left for the ablation.

### 3.2 Compute / memory cost (analytical estimates — verify with `torch.profiler` on your actual T4, these are back-of-envelope FLOP/byte counts, not measured wall-clock)

For a 3×3 deformable/aligned conv at two representative pyramid levels (batch = 1):

| Feature map | Main conv MACs (≈ same as a regular 3×3 conv, C_in=C_out) | Activation memory (fp32) | Offset field memory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64×64×128 | 64·64·128·128·9 ≈ **0.60 GMAC** (~1.2 GFLOP) | 64·64·128·4 B ≈ **2.1 MB** | 64·64·2·4 B ≈ 32 KB (per offset group) | Bilinear sampling adds ~4× memory *reads* per output pixel (negligible extra FLOPs, small extra memory bandwidth) |
| 32×32×256 | 32·32·256·256·9 ≈ **0.60 GMAC** (~1.2 GFLOP) | 32·32·256·4 B ≈ **1.05 MB** | 32·32·2·4 B ≈ 8 KB | Same ballpark FLOPs as the 64×64×128 level — typical of feature pyramids where channel count doubles as spatial resolution halves |

Both levels are trivial on a T4 (8.1 TFLOPS fp32 / 65 TFLOPS fp16 tensor-core) — a handful of these adds well under 1 ms per forward pass; the dominant cost in practice will be kernel-launch overhead from `grid_sample`/`deform_conv2d`, not FLOPs or bandwidth. **If** you add a correlation/cost-volume layer instead of/alongside direct offset regression:
- **Local correlation** (max displacement *d*, i.e. a (2d+1)×(2d+1) search window) at 64×64×128 with d=4: cost ≈ 64·64·128·81 ≈ **42.5 MMAC** — cheap; memory for the volume ≈ 64·64·81·4 B ≈ **1.3 MB**.
- **Global/dense correlation** (LoFTR/DKM-style, every location vs. every location) at 64×64 (4096 tokens): cost ≈ 4096² · 128 ≈ **2.1 GMAC**, volume memory ≈ 4096²·4 B ≈ **64 MB** — still fine on a T4 at this one coarse resolution, but **do not** run global correlation at 128×128 or above (16384² · C blows past reasonable memory/compute; this is exactly why LoFTR/DKM only ever do dense matching at 1/8–1/16 resolution and refine locally afterward). **Practical recommendation**: local correlation with a small *d* (2–4 px, matching your expected registration error) is enough for this project's stated misregistration magnitude; global correlation is unnecessary machinery that invites reviewer questions about why you need it.

### 3.3 Gradient-stability gotchas (all verified against current PyTorch docs/issues)

- **`grid_sample(align_corners=...)`**: PyTorch's own docs state the default changed and that "the default behavior will change to `align_corners=False` … avoid using it and explicitly specify"; with `align_corners=True`, sampled locations depend on feature-map resolution, which breaks consistent behavior across your feature pyramid levels (a 64×64 and 32×32 warp with the same normalized offset will not correspond to the same physical displacement unless you handle this carefully). **Always pass `align_corners=False` explicitly**, and if you resize the offset field between pyramid levels, resize with `F.interpolate(..., align_corners=False)` too, matching the flag (PyTorch docs explicitly say "whichever option is used here should also be used" in `interpolate`).
- **`padding_mode`**: `'zeros'` will pull in black/zero features for any offset that pushes a sample off the feature-map edge — this creates a spurious "change" signal at the image border. `'border'` (clamp-to-edge) is safer for small bounded offsets like yours. Also note the historical PyTorch issue that `'border'`/`'reflection'` padding modes had **incorrect (later fixed) gradients at the border** (pytorch/pytorch#23925) — use a reasonably recent PyTorch (≥1.5) where this is fixed.
- **Zero-init the last offset-predicting conv layer** (weight and bias both zero). This is the standard trick from the DCN/BasicVSR line: at initialization, `tanh(0)=0` ⇒ offset ≡ 0 ⇒ your alignment module starts as a pure identity warp, so early training behaves exactly like the *no-alignment* baseline and only deviates from it once the offset head has learned something useful. Skipping this risks large random early offsets corrupting the feature pyramid before the rest of the network has learned anything (this is essentially what Chan et al. 2021, arXiv:2009.07265, call "offset overflow").
- **Learning-rate scaling for offset branches**: standard practice in DCN-based video restoration work (EDVR, BasicVSR family) is to use a **reduced LR multiplier (commonly ~0.1×)** specifically for offset-predicting convolutions relative to the rest of the network, because offsets are high-sensitivity, low-dimensional outputs that can oscillate or diverge under the same LR that is stable for the (much higher-dimensional, more averaged) main feature convolutions. Implement via per-parameter-group `lr` in your optimizer (`torch.optim.Adam([{'params': base_params}, {'params': offset_params, 'lr': base_lr * 0.1}])`).
- **`tanh` saturation**: if `max_disp` is set too large relative to typical needed offsets, gradients through `tanh` saturate near ±1 and the offset head trains slowly; if `max_disp` is set too small, you architecturally cannot correct larger misregistration even if the network wants to. Pick `max_disp` empirically from your dataset's actual registration-error distribution (measure it directly — e.g., via classical keypoint matching residuals on your reference/edited pairs — rather than guessing), and treat it as a reported hyperparameter, not an afterthought; a reviewer will ask how you chose it.

## 4. Symmetry / order invariance in Siamese networks — the core of contribution (B)

### 4.1 Precise taxonomy of fusion functions and their symmetry class

Let `a = Enc(I1)`, `b = Enc(I2)` (shared weights ⇒ `Enc` itself is order-agnostic *as a function*, but the *fusion* of its two outputs is where symmetry is won or lost). For fusion function `φ(a,b)` feeding a head `h(·)`, define the model's order-swap behavior as `f(I1,I2) = h(φ(a,b))` vs. `f(I2,I1) = h(φ(b,a))`.

| Fusion `φ(a,b)` | Symmetry class | Consequence for `f(I2,I1)` vs `f(I1,I2)` | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| `\|a−b\|` (abs diff) | **Symmetric**: `φ(a,b)=φ(b,a)` | `f(I1,I2) = f(I2,I1)` **exactly**, automatically, no loss needed | FC-Siam-diff (Daudt, Le Saux, Boulch, "Fully Convolutional Siamese Networks for Change Detection," ICIP 2018, arXiv:1810.08462) |
| `a+b`, `max(a,b)`, `‖a−b‖` (distance/metric) | **Symmetric** | Same — exact, free invariance | STANet (Chen & Shi, "A Spatial-Temporal Attention-Based Method and a New Dataset for Remote Sensing Image Change Detection," *Remote Sensing* 2020, 12(10):1662, DOI: 10.3390/rs12101662) uses a learned metric/distance space, which is symmetric by construction |
| `concat(a,b)` | **Neither** — no algebraic relationship between `φ(a,b)` and `φ(b,a)` | `f(I1,I2)` and `f(I2,I1)` are *unconstrained relative to each other*; a plain `Conv(concat(a,b))` head has no reason to satisfy any swap identity at all | FC-Siam-conc (Daudt et al. 2018, above); most concat+attention transformer CD heads (ChangeFormer) unless explicitly symmetrized |
| **`a−b` (signed difference)** | **Antisymmetric**: `φ(b,a) = −φ(a,b)` | `f(I1,I2)=h(a−b)`, `f(I2,I1)=h(b−a)=h(−(a−b))`. These are equal **iff `h` happens to be an even function of its input** (`h(−z)=h(z)`). A generic learned `h` (a conv stack ending in sigmoid) is *not* even — nothing in training forces it to be — so **order-consistency is not automatic and must be explicitly learned or architecturally imposed.** | **Your project.** This is exactly the mechanism you should state precisely in your proposal (see box below). |

> **The precise argument to put in your proposal, stated formally:** Weight-sharing in a Siamese encoder gives you `Enc(I1)=a`, `Enc(I2)=b` regardless of which image is "I1," which is necessary but *not sufficient* for order-invariant output. Invariance additionally requires the **fusion+head composition to be an even function of the ordered pair**. For symmetric fusions (`|a−b|`, `max`, `a+b`) this holds for *any* head `h`, because the potentially-antisymmetric information (which one is "the reference") is destroyed before `h` ever sees it — which is exactly why those architectures *cannot* natively predict a direction. Your architecture deliberately keeps `a−b` (signed) so the direction information *survives* into `h`, which is the right design choice for a directional appeared/disappeared head — but it is precisely this choice that makes order-consistency of the *binary* branch a property you must now enforce with a loss (or a further architectural constraint), not something you get for free. This is the crux fact reviewers must see you understand.

### 4.2 Is "pair-order consistency" already published under another name?

Search terms tried: "bitemporal symmetry," "temporal symmetry consistency change detection," "exchange invariance siamese," "order-invariant change detection," "commutative change detection," "swap consistency." Findings, most → least direct:

1. **ChangeMask** (Zheng, Zhong, Tian, Ma, Zhang, "ChangeMask: Deep multi-task encoder-transformer-decoder architecture for semantic change detection," *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, vol. 183, 2022, pp. 228–239, DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2021.10.021). Explicitly names **"temporal symmetry"** as one of two inductive biases the architecture is built around (the other being the semantic→change causal relationship), and introduces a **Time-Symmetric Transformer (TST)** specifically to "guarantee temporal symmetry for change representation and keep it discriminative." **This is your single closest prior-art citation for contribution (B)'s binary branch** and must be discussed and differentiated explicitly, not just cited in passing. Key differentiation you can honestly claim: (i) ChangeMask bakes symmetry in *architecturally* for the binary change branch and handles direction *only* via two independent per-frame semantic segmentation heads (see §5) — it never trains an explicit, fused, direction-classifying head, so it never confronts the antisymmetric-fusion problem at all; (ii) it targets closed-set land-cover semantics (SECOND/HRSCD), not generic RGB scene edits.
2. **"Exchange Is All You Need for Remote Sensing Change Detection"** (Dong, Fu, Li, Cao, Meng, Du; arXiv:2601.07805, posted January 2026 — i.e., roughly contemporaneous with your project, not yet peer-reviewed at time of writing). Proposes **SEED** (Siamese Encoder-Exchange-Decoder): replaces differencing with a **parameter-free feature exchange**, formalized as "an orthogonal permutation operator," claimed to "preserve mutual information and Bayes-optimal risk." This is architectural (built into the forward pass), not a training-time consistency *loss*, and — as far as the retrievable abstract/description indicates — targets the binary change map only, not a directional appeared/disappeared head. **Because this is so recent and so close in spirit, you must cite it and explicitly differentiate**: your contribution is a *loss-based* consistency mechanism compatible with an *arbitrary* (including antisymmetric/signed) fusion trunk, extended to a 2-class direction head with a swap-and-permute target — SEED's permutation-operator argument is (by its own framing) about a parameter-free symmetric architecture, which sidesteps rather than solves the signed-fusion/antisymmetry problem you're targeting. Treat this paper as required reading before you finalize your proposal wording; if it turns out (on full read) to already cover the directional case, that materially changes your novelty claim and you should pivot immediately — flag this to your supervisor as a risk to check in week 1, not week 10.
3. **"Exchange is all you need" (older sense — pixel-consistency in Siamese CD encoders)**: prior to the Jan 2026 paper above, "exchange" already had currency in the CD literature as a **zero-parameter permutation operator applied to intermediate features under a "pixel-consistency principle"** (as long as spatial correspondence is preserved, the change label shouldn't change) — this is architectural feature-swapping *inside* the encoder to improve cross-temporal information flow (closer to mutual-attention/cross-fusion papers like MASNet, arXiv:2206.02331), not a swap-of-*input*-order consistency loss. Don't conflate the two "exchange" usages in your related work — cite carefully.
4. No hits for "commutative change detection" or "swap consistency" as established terms of art — these appear to be free for you to coin/define precisely (a mild positive for novelty framing: you're not stepping on an existing name).
5. **Consistency regularization more broadly** (Mean Teacher, FixMatch) is used in CD, but for **semi-supervised label propagation**, not order-swap consistency: Tarvainen & Valpola, "Mean teachers are better role models," NeurIPS 2017 (EMA teacher/student); Sohn et al., "FixMatch: Simplifying Semi-Supervised Learning with Consistency and Confidence," NeurIPS 2020; applied to CD e.g. in GTPC-SSCD ("Gate-guided Two-level Perturbation Consistency-based Semi-Supervised Change Detection," arXiv:2411.18880) and SemiCD-VL (arXiv:2405.04788). These enforce consistency across **augmentation/perturbation** of the *same-order* input pair to exploit unlabeled data — a different axis from yours (order permutation of a *fully labeled* pair), but worth one sentence in related work to pre-empt "isn't this just consistency regularization" — your answer: yes, same family of idea (perturb → require consistent output), but the specific perturbation (swap the two frames) is a *problem-structural symmetry* your task actually has (unlike, say, random crop), which is what makes it a meaningful architectural/loss target rather than a generic SSL trick, and unlike Mean Teacher/FixMatch it needs no unlabeled data or teacher network at all — it's a fully-supervised structural regularizer.
6. **Cycle-consistency / equivariance in image matching**: forward–backward flow consistency (MirrorFlow, DF-Net, above) and cycle-consistent correspondence learning (e.g., "Self-Supervised Partial Cycle-Consistency for Multi-View Matching," arXiv:2501.06000) are the closest *methodological* cousins outside CD — same idea (apply an operation and its inverse/dual, penalize deviation from identity), different object (spatial correspondence, not class-label direction). Cite as the general pattern your loss instantiates in the classification-direction setting rather than the flow setting.

### 4.3 What the loss must therefore enforce (given signed-difference fusion)

Given the antisymmetry established in §4.1, define your two heads precisely:

- **Binary change head** `p_change = σ(h_bin(a−b))`. Consistency target: `p_change(I1,I2) ≈ p_change(I2,I1)`. Loss: `L_swap-bin = D(p_change(I1,I2), p_change(I2,I1))` for a symmetric divergence `D` (e.g., squared error or symmetrized KL/BCE-consistency, both directions through stop-gradient on one branch per step to avoid the trivial `h_bin ≡ const` collapse — a real risk: minimizing only the consistency term with no task loss is satisfied by any constant output, so `L_swap-bin` must always be a secondary term added to, never substituted for, the supervised segmentation loss).
- **Directional head** `[p_nochange, p_appeared, p_disappeared] = softmax(h_dir(a−b))`. Consistency target is **not equality but permutation**: swapping input order must swap the appeared/disappeared channels while leaving no-change fixed: `p_appeared(I1,I2) ≈ p_disappeared(I2,I1)`, `p_disappeared(I1,I2) ≈ p_appeared(I2,I1)`, `p_nochange(I1,I2) ≈ p_nochange(I2,I1)`. This gives you a **fully self-supervised, ground-truth-free diagnostic metric** you can compute even on unlabeled or held-out data — report it as an evaluation metric (e.g., "swap-consistency KL," "direction-swap accuracy") for *every* baseline in your ablation table, not only your own model, since it's a fair, cheap, universal probe of a real failure mode.
- **Hard vs. soft enforcement — mention both, implement the soft one**: A *soft* auxiliary loss (above) is what a semester project should implement — cheap, compatible with any backbone, ablatable by simply toggling the loss weight. A *hard* architectural guarantee is possible for the binary branch by forcing `h_bin` to be even by construction, e.g. `h_bin(z) := g(z) + g(−z)` for an arbitrary learned `g` — this guarantees exact invariance regardless of training, and makes a nice "upper-bound" ablation row showing how close the soft loss gets to the hard guarantee. The directional head cannot be made "hard-symmetric" in the same trivial way (you don't want evenness, you want an odd/permutation-equivariant structure between two specific channels) — note in your write-up that this is a place where architectural tricks run out and a learned loss is the more natural (not just more convenient) solution, which is a nice thing to say explicitly to a reviewer who wonders "why not just fix this architecturally too."

## 5. Directional / semantic change detection — how existing work actually handles order

Datasets:
- **SECOND** (Yang, Xia, Liu, Du, Yang, Pelillo, Zhang, "Semantic Change Detection with Asymmetric Siamese Networks," arXiv:2010.05687) — 4,662 bitemporal pairs, 512×512, 0.5–3 m/px, one no-change + 6 land-cover classes, introduces the **Separated Kappa (SeK)** metric to handle class imbalance.
- **HRSCD** (Daudt, Le Saux, Boulch, Gousseau, "Multitask Learning for Large-scale Semantic Change Detection," arXiv:1810.08452; also *Computer Vision and Image Understanding*, 2019) — 291 pairs of 10,000×10,000 px, 0.5 m/px aerial RGB, 5 land-cover classes, rasterized from Urban Atlas 2006/2012.

Directional models:
- **BiSRNet** (Ding, Guo, Liu, Mou, Zhang, Bruzzone, "Bi-Temporal Semantic Reasoning for the Semantic Change Detection in HR Remote Sensing Images," **IEEE TGRS, vol. 60, 2022, pp. 1–14**, arXiv:2108.06103) — a 3-branch CNN (two temporal semantic branches + one change branch), with `Siam-SR` and `Cot-SR` semantic-reasoning blocks improving communication between the temporal and change branches.
- **ChangeMask** (§4.2) — decouples SCD into (i) per-time semantic segmentation (each frame classified *independently* through the shared-weight encoder) and (ii) a symmetric binary change branch via TST; "from→to" transition labels are read off by **pairing the two independent per-frame semantic predictions post-hoc**, not by training a dedicated learned "direction" classifier.
- **ChangeStar / ChangeMixin** (Zheng, Zhong, Zheng, Ma, Zhang, "Change is Everywhere: Single-Temporal Supervised Object Change Detection in Remote Sensing Imagery," ICCV 2021, arXiv:2108.07002; successor "ChangeStar2" is a unified model reported to be "slightly better than ChangeMask overall, but worse on the binary CD sub-task") — reuses an off-the-shelf semantic segmentation backbone via a plug-in ChangeMixin module; trained partly with *unpaired* single-temporal images as a change-augmentation trick, so it does not depend on a bespoke bitemporal architecture for direction at all.
- **SSCDL** ("Remote Sensing Image Semantic Change Detection Boosted by Semi-Supervised Contrastive Learning of Semantic Segmentation," IEEE TGRS, 2024) — noted in the literature to under-detect change regions (recall issue), evaluated on Landsat-SCD; same "independent per-frame semantic classification, then pair" pattern.

**The key structural fact for your novelty argument**: essentially every SCD paper surveyed here obtains its "from→to" direction *for free*, without ever training an antisymmetric or signed-fusion "direction head," because they classify each time step **independently** into a fixed, closed-set land-cover taxonomy and then simply read off the ordered pair `(class_at_t1, class_at_t2)`. Swapping input order in that paradigm trivially and automatically swaps which label is "from" and which is "to" — there is no learned function that could get this wrong, so **the antisymmetric-fusion consistency problem you are solving simply does not arise in the SCD literature as surveyed**, because none of these architectures fuse the two time steps into a single signed representation before making the directional call. That is your cleanest wedge: **your setting requires a genuinely different formulation** because (a) you are not assuming a fixed closed-set semantic taxonomy per frame (you want binary appeared/disappeared for arbitrary scene edits, not "building→road"), and (b) determining "did this specific region appear or disappear" is inherently a *pairwise*, not per-frame, judgment when there is no external semantic label to hang it on — so a fused, signed representation feeding a direction head is the natural design, and *that* is exactly the design for which order-consistency stops being free and must be explicitly learned. State this contrast explicitly; it is a genuinely good, correct, citable argument, not just a rhetorical move.

## 6. Signed pairwise fusion — literature and trade-offs

- **Classical remote sensing already treats "sign" as "direction" formally**: Change Vector Analysis (CVA) computes a per-pixel difference vector between two multispectral observations; its **magnitude** gives change strength and its **direction/sign** gives the *type* of spectral change. Foundational: Malila, W.A., "Change vector analysis: an approach for detecting forest changes with Landsat," *Proc. LARS Symposia*, 1980. Modern theoretical treatment: Bovolo & Bruzzone, "A Theoretical Framework for Unsupervised Change Detection Based on Change Vector Analysis in the Polar Domain," **IEEE TGRS, vol. 45, no. 1, 2007** — explicitly reparametrizes the (signed) difference vector into polar coordinates (magnitude = "did it change," angle = "what kind of change/which direction"), which is conceptually exactly your appeared/disappeared split, just from decades earlier and in a hand-engineered rather than learned setting. **Cite this as deep intellectual precedent** for "sign encodes direction" — it substantially strengthens your motivation section and shows you know the field didn't start with deep learning.
- **Deep CD fusion practice mostly discards sign deliberately**: FC-Siam-diff and most Siamese CD backbones use `|a−b|` specifically *because* they only need magnitude (binary change), and note runtime/robustness benefits of doing so (order invariance for free, per §4.1). Papers using raw `a−b` (signed, no abs) for CD are less common precisely because most binary-CD papers don't need direction — this is itself evidence that keeping the sign is a deliberate, non-default choice with a real cost (losing free invariance) that you must justify with the payoff (able to predict direction, richer supervisory signal) — which you can now do, citing §4 directly.
- **Trade-off summary for your methods section**: `|a−b|` → cheap exact order-invariance, zero directional information, cannot support an appeared/disappeared head at all. `a−b` (signed) → directional information preserved, but order-invariance of any *downstream binary decision* must now be earned via a loss/architecture (§4.3), and the antisymmetric channel is more sensitive to noise/registration error than its magnitude (small misalignments can flip apparent sign in a way they wouldn't flip magnitude) — which is precisely why contribution (A)'s alignment module and contribution (B)'s consistency loss are *complementary*, not independent, contributions: better alignment reduces spurious sign flips at boundaries, and the swap-consistency loss forces the network not to *rely* on spurious sign asymmetries that alignment failed to remove. **This complementarity argument is worth a sentence in your introduction** — it's a real interaction, not just "we also did a second thing."
